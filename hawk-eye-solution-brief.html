Solution Brief Team Hawk

Hawk Eye — Smart Road Intelligence Platform

A dashcam-powered, Google Maps add-on that transforms every vehicle into a real-time road intelligence node
Version 1.0  |  November 2026  |  Confidential

Problem Statement

Urban and inter-city road networks suffer from reactive information gaps. Drivers lack real-time, ground-truth data about road conditions ahead, leading to preventable accidents, inefficient routing, delayed emergency response, and unaddressed traffic violations. Existing map platforms aggregate user reports manually and intermittently — they do not provide continuous, automated, sensor-grade intelligence at scale.

⚠
Road Hazards & Conditions Potholes, waterlogging, construction zones, and debris go unreported until after incidents occur.
🚑
Accidents & Emergencies Delayed notification to police and hospitals increases severity of outcomes.
🚗
Traffic Jams No proactive, sensor-verified congestion data for dynamic rerouting decisions.
🔒
Illegal Parking & Violations Traffic departments lack automated evidence collection to act on violations efficiently.

Proposed Solution

Hawk Eye is a cloud-connected dashcam platform built by Team Hawk that turns every enrolled vehicle into a continuously sensing road intelligence node. It operates as a plug-in layer on top of Google Maps, enriching its navigation graph with verified, real-time sensor data. The system closes the loop between detection, notification, routing, and enforcement.

Key Integrations

  • Google Maps Platform — Routes API, Maps JavaScript API overlays for hazard and congestion layers
  • Emergency Services — RESTful API or PSAP gateway integration for auto-dispatch notifications
  • Traffic Department Portals — Challan / violation evidence submission via government API endpoints
  • Fleet Management Systems — Webhook / SDK integration with Fleetx, Locus, or custom TMS platforms
  • Insurance Providers — Telematics data feed for usage-based insurance (UBI) programmes

Privacy & Compliance

  • All video and imagery anonymised (face/plate blurring) before storage and sharing, except in verified emergency or enforcement submissions
  • Data residency controls to comply with regional data localisation laws (e.g., India DPDPA, GDPR)
  • Role-based access control (RBAC) — authorities receive only the data class they are authorised for
  • Subscriber opt-in consent model; data contribution is transparent and auditable
  • AES-256 encryption at rest and TLS 1.3 in transit
